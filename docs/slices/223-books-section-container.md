# Slice 223 — Books: a section as a container

Status: **coded (2026-10-23)**, see "Verified" for what ran and what did not. Dependencies: Triples (slices 103 and 104: `Statements`, `listing()`, the clean-up on deletion), slice 219 (how the editor scripts of Books are loaded). Module: `Books`. Reasoning: [`../design/books.md`](../design/books.md) ("The definition of a book: sections, units and kinds", "A section is a post, not a new post type").

## Goal

Let Eric create a **section** and **attach posts to it** as he prepares them, with nothing else: no PDF of the section, no merge, no numbering, no table of contents. The section is only a **container**, which can stay a draft while it holds posts that are already published. The attachment can be made **from the section** ("add these posts") and **from a post** ("put this post in that section").

## What it does

1. **A section is any post or page that is marked as one.** The mark is a statement `(post, books/is-section, true)` of Triples (not a post meta: the sections are then found through the index `(object_type, object_id, predicate)` of the statements, nothing is added to the meta table, the mark goes with the post when it is deleted and no `phpcs:ignore` is needed). It is set by a switch "Use this post as a section" in the panel, and **automatically** by the first addition to the section. The mark is needed because a **new section is empty**: without it nothing could list it, and the choice "in which section?" would have to scan every post. The types are `post` and `page` (filter `books_section_post_types`), whatever the status.
2. **The relation `books/contains`**, registered by Books (action `triples_register_predicates`): subject and object are posts, ordered by `triples/position` (built in). No qualifier yet (the modes and `books/pages` of the example of `usage-examples.md` come with later slices). A post can be in **several** sections. The statements follow the deletion of a post (Triples' clean-up), and Books takes the post out of its sections just **before** (`before_delete_post`, priority 9) so that the positions of the other posts stay right (see 4).
3. **The order** is the natural order of Triples' `listing()`: **the date of the post, oldest first** (then the id); a position (`triples/position` on the statement) overrides it. Adding a post gives it no position: it appears at its date among the posts that were not moved.
4. **Moving.** `up` and `down` exchange the post with its neighbour, `to=<rank>` puts it at a rank (from 1; to its own rank nothing happens; outside the list it is refused). **Every post whose rank changes gets a position** (the posts of the range between the old and the new rank), so that the result is exact whatever positions the other posts already have; the posts outside the range keep their place. Found on a real WordPress: giving a position to the moved post only left the posts that an earlier exchange had pinned where they were, and the displaced post at the end. **Taking a post out** gives the posts that had a position the rank they now have (a rank beyond the end of the list would put them in another order: the order stays the old one without the post). **Reset the order** removes every position.
5. **The panel "Section"** in the sidebar of the post editor (plain JavaScript like the preview panel, tested with jsdom), for the types above:
   - **In sections**: the sections that contain this post, each with its title (a link to its editor) and **Remove** (shown only for the sections the user may edit; the panel then reads its own state again, because the answer of the route is the state of the section); a field **Add to a section** that searches **among the sections only**, as the user types (debounced, 20 results, the most recently modified first when the field gets the focus empty), offers neither the post itself nor the sections it is in, and adds the post at the end of the chosen one.
   - **Use this post as a section** (the switch of point 1); when on, **Contains (n)**: the ordered list (title, date, status, "moved" when it has a position), with **Up**, **Down** (disabled at the ends), **Remove** and **Reset the order** (disabled when nothing was moved), and a button **Add posts…**.
   - **Add posts…** opens a **dialog** (plain DOM, `role="dialog"`, closed by its button and by Escape): a text search, a **From / To** date range, the statuses (publish by default, then draft, pending, private, future), the results 50 at a time with a **More results** button and **check boxes** (those already in the section are shown ticked and cannot be changed), **Add the selection** and **Add all the results** (every page, at most 200 at a time, those already in the section left out). It stays open to add more and reports "n added, m already there, k skipped".
6. **Finding a section from a post must not scan the posts.** Both lookups use the statements: *which sections contain this post* is `subjects_of( $post, 'books/contains' )`, the index `(object_type, object_id, predicate)`; *which sections exist* is `subjects_of( true, 'books/is-section' )`, the same index. The text search then asks `WP_Query` for **those ids only** (`post__in`, at most 1000, `s`, 20 per page, capped at 50): never a listing of every post.
7. **Rules enforced on the server** (the browser is never trusted):
   - `edit_post` on the **section** to add, remove, move, reset, mark, unmark or ask for candidates; `read_post` on each post added (an unreadable one is **skipped and reported**, not an error);
   - a section **cannot contain itself or one of the sections that contain it** (no cycle, however deep): the post is **skipped with the reason `cycle`** and the panel says so; a shortcut that is not a cycle is accepted;
   - a post is not added twice (reported as "already there", not an error); a missing post (`missing`), a post of another type (`type`) and an unreadable one (`forbidden`) are skipped and reported; the other posts of the batch are still added (one transaction);
   - at most 200 ids per request (`books_section_too_many`, 400); the page and the number per page are capped;
   - unmarking a section that still contains posts is refused (`books_section_not_empty`, 409).
8. **REST routes** (`books/v1`, `permission_callback` and the REST cookie nonce, a message that can be translated for each refusal): `GET sections?search=&page=` (needs `edit_posts`: the sections the user may edit); `GET` and `PUT sections/<id>` (the state; mark or unmark with `section=true|false`); `POST sections/<id>/contents` (`ids[]`, answers the report and the state); `DELETE sections/<id>/contents/<post>`; `POST sections/<id>/contents/<post>/move` (`to=up|down|<rank>`); `DELETE sections/<id>/order`; `GET sections/<id>/candidates?search=&after=&before=&statuses[]=&page=` (50 per page, those already in the section flagged, the section itself left out). All but the first need `edit_post` on `<id>`. The state is `{ id, section, contents[ { id, title, status, type, date, edit, pinned } ], containing[ { …, can_edit } ] }`; a refusal is a `WP_Error` with the code and the status of the slice's refusals (`books_section_not_found` 404, `_forbidden` 403, `_bad_type` 400, `_not_empty` 409, `_too_many` 400, `_not_in` 404, `_bad_move` 400).
9. **WP-CLI** (see `docs/wp-cli.md`): `wp otherguise books section list [--search=] [--format=table|json]`, `show <id> [--format=]`, `add <section> [<ids>…] [--search=] [--from=] [--to=] [--status=] [--dry-run]`, `remove <section> <ids>… [--dry-run]`. The same rules as the REST routes; a command line has no logged-in user, so its posts are those of a store that does not ask the capabilities. Each can be run twice.
10. **Clean-up**: nothing of its own to remove: the statements belong to Triples (its uninstall removes the table when the administrator asked for it).

## What it does not do

- No kind of unit (day, opener, summary…), no page counts, no templates for a section: later slices ([design](../design/books.md), "kinds").
- No PDF of a section, no merge, no folios, no table of contents or index.
- No private link (slice 400): the container works on drafts and published posts of the same site as the editor.
- No automatic content (a section filled by a rule such as a range of dates): the posts are chosen.
- The panel is a sidebar panel and a dialog, not a floating panel as the preview; to see after use whether a floating panel is wanted.
- When a post is **moved to the trash** it stays in its section (it is deleted for good only when emptied from the trash).

## Verified

- **Automated**: 502 PHPUnit tests (with MariaDB 10.11.14) and 89 jsdom tests pass; `phpcs` (WordPress standard) reports nothing on the new code except the file-naming rule that the whole plugin breaks (PSR-4 names).
- **Real WordPress 7.1.3 (scratch site, 2026-10-23)**:
  - **WP-CLI**: `section add 311 --from= --to=` (4 added, then run twice: nothing), `show`, `list`, `list --format=json`.
  - **REST** through `rest_do_request` with real requests: refusals for a subscriber (403), search, add with a draft, itself and a missing post (reported), `down`, rank 1 and `up` on the first (400), reset, candidates, unmark of a non-empty section (409), remove, state of a day. This is where the first version of `move` was found wrong.
  - **The editor in Chromium (Playwright), logged in**: the panel in the sidebar of a page; the contents in order; Down and Reset; the dialog (filters, a draft ticked and added, the report); a day shows its section; add to another section by the search; leave it (the panel's own state; a first version showed the state of the section); switch a post into a section and back.
- **Never tried**: the production and testing sites of the blog; a real author with limited capabilities in the editor (the capabilities were tried through REST and unit tests); several users editing the same section; sections with hundreds of posts (the cap of 200 per request is tested, not the speed); the dialog on a phone.
